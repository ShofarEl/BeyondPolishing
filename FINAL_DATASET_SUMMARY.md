### The Canonical Problem (Used by ALL Participants)

**SEED PROBLEM:**
```
I want to propose a project to predict dorm Wi-Fi usage to help the 
university scale and prioritize its router upgrades. By analyzing the 
12 months of connection logs, I plan to build a time-series model to 
forecast which buildings will experience the highest bandwidth strain. 
I will look at connection timestamps, device types, and location data 
to pinpoint peak usage times across different dorms. The goal is to 
provide the IT department with a data-driven schedule to replace 
hardware exactly where it is needed most.
```

- Specify metrics (reduce latency by X%, increase throughput by Y Mbps)
- Define data requirements (connection logs + traffic data + signal strength + user feedback)

- Consider scalability for future expansion

**CHALLENGER RESPONSE** (Reframing-focused):
Three alternative framings:
1. **Equity-Focused**: Use fairness metrics instead of prediction accuracy to prioritize underserved dormitories
2. **Student Success**: Identify connectivity barriers affecting academic performance and well-being
3. **Sustainable Design**: Create adaptive network with dynamic bandwidth allocation to minimize hardware replacement

## 💡 Implications

### 1. **Both Modes Have Value**

### 2. **Sequence Matters**
Optimal approach: **Challenger → Editor**
1. Use Challenger first to expand problem space
2. Use Editor second to ensure feasibility

### 3. **Adaptive AI Systems**
Future AI tutors should:
- Detect student's phase (exploration vs refinement)
- Switch modes based on context
- Provide scaffolding for high cognitive load

- Advanced students: May benefit more from Challenger
- Novice students: May need Editor support first
- All students: Need both at different stages
